# The daily loop

The loop is short: plan the day, start the first step, close the day. Once a week there is a review.

## Plan the day

Open Home and tap **Plan the day**. A notification at your morning check-in time opens it too: it says what the day holds and the room it leaves ("Morning. Today has the 10:00 dentist and room for about 3 h. Plan it?"), or "A free day so far. Plan it?". It is a short morning ritual, one question per screen. With an AI set up, it opens a proposal instead (see **Plan the day with an AI** below), and the ritual is one tap away.

1. **Yesterday.** One line of what was played. Then each step left open is asked about on its own card, one at a time: **Today**, **Another day** (tomorrow, one of the next days, or **No date**: it leaves the day and its due date and stays on your list, not dropped), **Drop it** (a second tap confirms; it says what it unlocked) or **Make it a decision** (it opens the decision with the step). If it was done and not ticked, **Already done** asks how long it took. Nothing moves until you answer. A fixed-time thing from yesterday whose time passed is asked about the same way, **Move** (to another day or time) or **Remove**. Réussite says nothing about what didn't happen, and never counts it.
2. **Today's shape.** What kind of day is it (a usual day, afternoon only, an hour or less, a rest day)? **From when** and **until when**: the start begins at the next five minutes, and the end at the day's end (Settings → Method, 19:00 by default); this morning's answer stands in for that setting, today only. What is fixed today? If one of your places has areas, **Where will you be?** lets you pick one for the morning and one for the afternoon (see [Places and trips](places-and-trips.md)).
3. **The four pillars.** One row for each of work, papers, family and life, filled in by the method. Keep each one, **Swap** it for another of the same weight, or **Skip** it (**Keep it** brings it back). A row with nothing in it says so in one line ("No life step today. Fine, or add one?") and offers **Add one**.
4. **The plan.** The steps are laid out against the clock, with the time each is expected to start, between your fixed times. Under the list: "3 h 10 of 4 h 30 free", the steps' estimates (adjusted by your pace) next to the time you have between the start and the end of the day, minus what is fixed. When there is more than room, it says by how much ("About 50 min more than the time you have.") and **Cut one** takes you to the cutting. Tap **Swap** to pick something of the same weight, or **Cut** to take something off; it stays on your list for another day. Tap **Go with this** when you are happy.
5. **The first step alone.** Its starter, its estimate and **Start**. **Not yet** closes the ritual without starting the timer.

![The plan, made by the method's rules](shot:checkin)

What the plan contains:

- Steps that are fixed in time come first, with "Leave at…".
- Then the **Now** steps for the place you're in.
- Then exactly one **Protect** step.
- Then **Quick** steps, if there is room.
- At most three work steps (you can change the limit in Settings → Method).
- One obligation, with a time: leisure, going out or seeing people.
- If you said where you'll be in the afternoon, the steps of that area come together at the end of the list, and the morning's area first. The plan shows "Afternoon in Heliopolis" on them.
- A little time for the planning itself.

Réussite adds up the estimates and shows what the day will probably take for you.

There are no launch points or restart reminders: steps float between your fixed times, and the order is the one Today follows (see Start here below).

### Plan the day with an AI

When an AI is set up (Settings → AI, with a key on this device), **Plan the day** on Home and the morning notification open a proposal instead of the questions. The AI proposes and you choose; it does not ask you open questions. Before the first one, Réussite shows you what will be sent, as always.

You see your day in five parts, each item with **Skip** and, when there is something else to put in its place, **Swap**:

1. **What has to happen today.** Things at a fixed time (they stay, they are not a choice), steps that are due or urgent, steps from yesterday you still have, and the things you are waiting for that are due to be checked.
2. **Your main goals.** The one or two projects to move today, and for each the step to start with. **Swap** changes the step, or the project.
3. **One for each pillar.** What the AI would put in work, papers, family and life, under the names you gave them ("For family: call your mother, 15 min"). When your list has nothing for a pillar, it may write a new step; it is marked **New**.
4. **Where you are.** Given your place today, the steps that belong there.
5. **The day, against the time you have.** "3 h 10 of 4 h 30 free", updated as you skip and swap, and the step to start with. When there is more than the time, it says by how much and leaves out what it would cut first, struck out; **Keep it** brings one back.

A skipped step is only left off today's list; it stays on your list. To change the proposal in your own words, type in the box ("lighter on work, add a walk") and tap **Adjust**, or tap **Too much, cut it** or **Room for one more**. What you skipped is not offered again.

**Make the plan** shows the changes the way every AI change is shown, with a tick for each: the new steps, the broken-down ones, and the day's list with its first step. Nothing is written before you tick. Undo is on Home's receipt and in History. If the AI is unreachable you still get the five parts, made by the rules and without its wording, with a way to the ritual. **Plan it myself** opens the ritual at any time. With no AI set up, nothing changes.

## Deadlines, appointments and slots

A step can carry three different things, each in its own field in the step’s sheet:

- **Due** is a deadline, a day and, if you like, a time: it must be done by then. It never goes in the calendar and never takes the step off your list. After the time has passed today the step stays on Today and says “was due 17:00”, in the ordinary colour. After the day it says “carried over”, not “late”.
- **At** is an appointment, something that happens at that time: a call, a visit, a lunch. It goes in the calendar and the reminders. If its time passes and it is not done, it stays on Today marked **missed**, and a small card offers **Move** or **Remove**.
- **Slot** is time you set aside to work on the step: a day, a start and a length. Planning your day gives each planned step a slot. It can move, there is only one per step, and it is not an appointment. When it passes and the step is still open, the card says “slot passed” with **Re-slot**: that opens a proposed new time, and nothing moves until you confirm. The sheet keeps a short log of where the slot has been.

Typed on Home, “pay the rent by 17:00” or “avant 17h” sets a deadline time; a bare time, “lunch Monday 19:00”, sets an appointment. Slots come only from planning the day or the step’s sheet.

## Start

Today shows the first step on a card, **Start here**. It shows the step's starter: the smallest first action. Tap **Start the timer**, or **Already done** if you did it already. Forgot to start? **I started earlier** takes two taps. **Drop it**, tapped twice, drops a step that no longer matters; it waits in the Cemetery. Start here is always there: with nothing open on today's list, it shows the step your list would pick next, with **Start the timer**, **Put it first** and **Choose from your list**.

Today's top is kept short, so that Start here shows whole on a small phone, even on a Sunday with the review box above it. The date is in the top bar, with the time you have timed so far today beside it, small (left out when there is no room). Under it the day changer is one row: **‹ Today ›**. The arrows never move, so you can tap the same spot again and again to go a day at a time. Away from today the label is the day (“Wed 4 Nov”) and a small **↩ Today** chip beside it brings you back; tap the label to pick any date.

Today's list is the day's cards, and that includes the things at a fixed time: the dentist at 15:00 is a card on the list, with its time, and the same step is on the calendar. A change in one is a change in the other. **From your list**, **Leisure & people**, **Plan the day** and **I'm stuck** are above the list.

![Today: the first step and the rest of the list](shot:today)

**Start here follows the clock.** It is the first step that fits before your next fixed time, in the order of your list. A step that does not fit the gap waits for the next one, and a shorter step further down moves up; a step is never cut in two. When the next thing at a fixed time gets close, it takes over: Start here becomes that item, with its time and when to leave. That happens when the step you are on (what is left of it if the timer is running, or its estimate if not), plus a margin of a fifth of its length and at least 5 minutes, would run into the time you need to leave. A step with no estimate counts as 15 minutes. Something at a fixed time whose time has passed leaves the order, and Home and Today show one small card for it, "“Call the bank” was at 10:00", with **Move** (to another day or time) and **Remove** (a second tap confirms; a repeating one skips this time). **Already done** is there too. It is never red, and it is asked one at a time. Today's card and list follow this one order. Today's list shows when each step is expected to start ("≈10:40"); that is a projection that moves with the clock, not a promise. Dragging a step by its grip still sets the order of your list. Things at a fixed time keep their time, wherever you drop them.

**Done steps fold.** Under the list, the steps you ticked off are gathered in a row, "3 done ▸". Tap it, or press Enter on it, to see them; they are closed by default. Projects fold their done steps the same way.

Adding a step by hand to today (the round **+** button, **Add to today**, **From your list**) puts it right after the step you are timing, or before the first one not done yet. It doesn't wait at the end behind the whole plan.

You can drag a step by its grip to put the list in another order. The arrows still work. When a step has areas, the list is grouped under the area's name.

When you finish a step that others were waiting for, the next one takes its place right after it on the list.

If you gave something you are waiting for a day to be asked about, a **Check: …** row appears on Today from that day, and stays until you answer. **It happened** asks which day and frees the steps behind it. **Still waiting** asks again in 3 days, in a week or on a day you pick. **Drop it** gives up waiting. It is never a card on the list, and with no day it never comes by itself.

If a step comes with an app or a link, the button opens it for you and starts the timer.

## Pause and stuck

Tap **Pause** to stop: one tap, nothing to answer. A step with time already logged offers **Continue**, not Start: the clock picks up from the total, and each run stays its own line in the step's sessions. Marking such a step done never asks how long it took. The time so far is saved, and the step keeps its "where I stopped" note.

Tap **I'm stuck** when it won't start. Réussite asks which friction it is:

- **Can't start.** It's there and nothing happens.
- **Stopped and can't get back in.** There was a break and now it won't restart.
- **Drifted.** Attention went elsewhere.

Then it shows the starter, or helps you write a smaller one. If the same step stalls twice, it probably hides a decision or missing information. Réussite asks you to turn it into a question to someone, or a decision.

## Break it down

A step over 30 minutes is too big. Tap **Break it down**: it asks for the first action (the app or place, then what to do there), its starter, a time, and what comes next. It then asks whether the steps go **In this order** or **In any order**. In this order, each step waits for the one before, and they stay together in one card: you see the first, with "+3 after" under it that unfolds the rest. In any order, they are separate steps. See [Timing and estimates](timing-and-estimates.md) for the starter test.

## Close the day

Tap **Close the day**. Your cards are dealt out one by one, then you see the day's summary. Under Settings → Appearance you can switch the celebration off, calm or lively; with it off you go straight to the summary.

![The close of the day: the pillars played, the bar that moved, tomorrow's first step](shot:close)

The summary has:

- The cards you played and the time you logged.
- **One line for your pillars**, such as "♠ 2 · ♦ 1 · ♥ 1": the symbol of each pillar you played in, with how many cards. A pillar you didn't play in is left out.
- **The bars that moved today.** A project's bar moves when a step that moves its goal was done today, or a milestone was ticked today. Each shows its bar and what today added ("4 of 12 pages · +2 today"). Cards alone never move a bar, and steps still open count for nothing and are not mentioned.
- **Tomorrow's first step.** Pick one from your list, or write a new one; the summary says "Tomorrow starts with: …", and, if you like, when to start it.

Réussite states facts ("4 cards, 2 h 10"). It never praises your character, and it has no streaks.

## The weekly review

Once a week (Sunday by default), Today and the [Matrix](matrix.md) show a **Start the review** box at the top, and the bell lists it. It takes about 45 minutes, one question at a time:

1. What changed between the quadrants?
2. What no longer matters?
3. Sort the inbox into projects.
4. Give each project a concrete next step and a SMART goal.
5. Pick the week's Protect steps and their days.
6. Name the week's obligations: people, going out, leisure.
7. Queue the decisions to make, with dates.
8. Check decisions whose date has come.

It ends with how your estimates compared with reality.

### The goals

After the next steps, each active project gets two screens.

- **Is this goal still right?** You see the goal in one sentence, its bar and its dates. **Still right** goes on. **Edit the goal** opens the goal editor in the screen. **Close it** asks whether the goal was **Reached** or you want to **Let it go**; either way the project is closed (you can reopen it in Projects), and its steps are not asked.
- **Which steps move this goal?** Each open step of the project, with **Moves the goal? Yes / No**. The buttons show the answer the step already has, so a step you answered last week stays as you left it; one you never answered says "Not said". Only the steps you say Yes to fill the bar, once they are done.

With an AI set up, **Start the review** (on the review day, from Home's bell, Today or the Matrix, and in Settings → Method as **Review the goals with the AI**) opens a short conversation about the goals instead, one project at a time. For each project the AI checks the goal against SMART (the sentence, the measure and the dates), tells you for each open step whether it belongs to the goal and whether it moves it, and proposes changes: a clearer sentence, a measure, other dates, steps to drop or to add. You keep or **Skip** each proposal, flip any step's **Yes / No** with a tap, and can say what to change ("make the goal smaller"). After the last project, **See the changes** shows one list on the review screen: tick what you want and nothing is written before that. **The rest of the review** then goes on with the usual questions, and **Go through the goals myself** opens the review by hand at any point. Without an AI nothing changes.
