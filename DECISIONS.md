- 2026-10-19 · Relay tests use `@cloudflare/vitest-plugin`, not `@cloudflare/vitest-pool-workers`. · The pool package is deprecated (renamed); the plugin supports Vitest 5 like the app, and audits clean.
- 2026-10-19 · The relay is its own npm project in `relay/`, importing `src/sync` and `src/data` from the app's tree; the root `npm test` and `typecheck` don't touch it (`npm run relay:test`, `relay:typecheck`). · One copy of the crypto, protocol and merge code (brief §10), and the Worker's types (workers-types, no DOM) stay apart from the app's.
- 2026-10-19 · Recovery key: Crockford base32, 32 key bytes plus a 3-byte CRC-32, 56 characters in 14 groups of 4. · Fits a QR code and a line of paper; the checksum catches any single typo (a 1 in 16 million miss for random errors) and the alphabet has no look-alikes.
- 2026-10-19 · The auth hash is SHA-256 of the secret's base64url text, not its bytes. · One canonical string on both sides; no base64 padding ambiguity.
- 2026-10-19 · Blobs are AES-GCM with the format byte and space id as authenticated data; format 1 plain JSON, 2 gzip. · A blob can't be moved to another space or have its compression flag flipped; gzip when the platform has it, readable either way.
- 2026-10-19 · No outbox: a device pushes every field whose clock carries its own node id and is above a watermark. · The clock is monotonic per device, so this is exactly the unpushed set, with nothing to keep in step with the documents. Imported fields (node `0`) are sent on the first sync only.
- 2026-10-19 · A pull that contains a document from a newer schema applies nothing and doesn't advance the sequence number. · The older app goes read-only (brief §11) without writing newer-shaped data into an older database; after the reload the same data is pulled again.
- 2026-10-19 · Blobs are stored on the relay as base64url text and never decoded there. · The relay costs almost no CPU per request (free plan: 10 ms); the price is a third more storage and transfer.
- 2026-10-19 · Caps: a batch blob 1 MiB, a snapshot 4 MiB; a snapshot is stored in 1 MB parts. · A D1 row holds 2 MB; a gzipped personal list is a few hundred KB, so 4 MiB leaves room for years. A device whose snapshot is too big keeps working with batches.
- 2026-10-19 · Rate limits: 60 writes a minute per space (counted on the space's own row, no extra write) and 120 a minute per IP (one D1 row per IP and minute, counting writes and failed sign-ins only). · The free plan allows 100,000 D1 row writes a day; reads are not counted, and a 256-bit secret can't be guessed anyway.
- 2026-10-19 · Reads on an unknown space answer 404 `unknown_space`; a wrong secret answers 403 and nothing is written. · A joining device can tell "nothing there yet" from "wrong key"; the space id is not secret enough to matter.
- 2026-10-19 · A device pushes a snapshot after 50 batches since the last one. · New devices catch up from one blob plus a few batches; the relay's storage stays small.
- 2026-10-19 · Copy diagnostics scrubs error text against every string in the current tasks, projects, places, decisions and stays (replaced by ‹…›), and drops URL queries; the log is kept in `local` (device-only, no schema bump). · Error messages can quote titles or notes; hiding all known strings is safer than guessing which fields matter.
- 2026-10-19 · Sync and push add lines to diagnostics through `registerDiagnostics(section, provider)`; until then the block says "sync: off" and "push: not set up". · The diagnostics module needs no knowledge of them.
- 2026-10-19 · A top-level error boundary in `main.tsx` shows a plain crash screen with Reload and Copy diagnostics. · The app had no error screen of its own.
- 2026-10-19 · The Dexie sync store lives in `src/data/syncstore.ts`; incoming documents are written by `putMerged` in store.ts, which re-merges each with the stored copy inside the transaction and throws in read-only mode. · An edit made while a round is folding can't be overwritten, and a round never saves its sequence number for writes that were refused.
- 2026-10-19 · Documents from an older schema are upgraded on arrival with the same migrations, and fields a migration would only add as defaults are left out. · Received documents are partial; a default (decision `status`) would tie with a real value at the oldest clock and could win.
- 2026-10-19 · A first device with nothing to send still pushes an empty batch, so its space exists and a joining device doesn't see "empty". · The relay creates a space on its first write.
- 2026-10-19 · If the relay has no space for a device that had already pulled from it, sync pauses ("deleted elsewhere") instead of pushing everything back. · Delete everywhere on one device must not be undone by another.
- 2026-10-19 · A key with a valid checksum but no list on the relay isn't refused: the joiner gets a warning and may go on. · It can't be told apart from a first device that hasn't synced; the checksum already catches typos.
- 2026-10-19 · After an import or a restore, the next round pushes every field (`full`), not only own ones. · Imported and restored fields carry other clocks; a duplicate push is harmless.
- 2026-10-19 · The relay address is a synced setting (`relayUrl`) defaulting to `VITE_RELAY_URL`, editable only while sync is off; https, or http on localhost. · Every device of a person uses one relay; a fork sets its own at build time. The built page's CSP names the build's relay origin and keeps `https:` for AI endpoints.
- 2026-10-19 · Triggers: start, page visible, 2 s after the last local write, network back; retries at 5 s ×3 up to 5 min; one round at a time. The device is marked seen on the relay at most every 10 minutes. · Brief §6; the relay's free plan counts D1 writes.
- 2026-10-19 · The backup reminder (sync off) comes 14 days after the first run, the last saved backup, or the last "Not now", whichever is latest. · A new person isn't nagged on day one.
- 2026-10-19 · An encrypted backup is the plain backup sealed like a blob with the space key; Restore detects it, and refuses it without the key. · One format of crypto to review; it only opens on a device that holds the key.
- 2026-10-19 · QR codes use the bundled `qrcode-generator` (SVG, alphanumeric mode) and `jsqr` (loaded only when the camera is used, when `BarcodeDetector` is missing), both pinned. · No third-party requests; the 56-character key fits a small code.
- 2026-10-19 · The e2e run starts the relay (`wrangler dev`, local D1) beside the app, which is built for `http://localhost:8787`. · Two real browser contexts can sync without any Cloudflare account.
- 2026-10-19 · The connector uses `@modelcontextprotocol/server` 2.x with the Agents SDK's `createMcpHandler`, not `@modelcontextprotocol/sdk`. · That version of `agents` (0.28.1) takes the 2.x server; the 1.x SDK is only an optional peer. Pinned: workers-oauth-provider 1.2.3, agents 0.28.1, server 2.3.1, zod 4.6.7 (one copy, aliased); `npm audit` clean; the bundle is about 360 KiB gzipped (limit 3 MiB).
- 2026-10-19 · **CPU: the connector does not fit the free plan's 10 ms for a list of the size in the brief.** `npm run relay:bench` (Node, V8 as in workerd): 1,500 steps, 3,000 sessions, 200 days, 40 projects, 20 decisions is 3.5 MB of JSON (474 KB stored). Reading it (base64, decrypt, gunzip, parse, fold 30 batches) takes about 22–28 ms; a whole `today` or `query_tasks` call about 32–34 ms; a one-step edit about 26 ms. The MCP layer is about 1 ms. 70% of the bytes are the per-field clocks. · Options for the product owner: (1) Workers Paid, $5 a month, no code change; (2) the app also uploads a small "connector view" blob next to its snapshots (open steps, near days, no clocks, precomputed estimate points: 0.4 MB, about 3 ms to read, so about 8 ms for a call; the connector writes partial documents and needs no clocks), a protocol addition on the app side; (3) the same without the clocks but with every step: 1.1 MB, about 7 ms to read, still over. Nothing was changed in the architecture.
- 2026-10-19 · No connector-owned cache of the merged state. · The brief asked for one so a call "decrypts one blob plus the batches since"; but the app's snapshot already is that blob (the app uploads one after 50 batches), the cache would be as big and as slow to parse, and refreshing it means gzip and encrypt of 3.5 MB, which costs more CPU than a read. A call reads the latest snapshot and the batches after it (`loadSpace`), like a new device. The seam is one function, so option 2 above replaces it without touching the tools.
- 2026-10-19 · The connector's tool answers are plain text lines, not JSON. · An AI reads the same compact lines the app already sends it; fewer tokens, nothing to parse.
- 2026-10-19 · Low-risk means one change that adds a step, ticks one done, or moves one date (only `due`, or `due` with `at`). It is `isLowRisk` in `src/ai/apply.ts`, so the app's Home can use the same rule. · Brief §2 and §10 name the three; a change to a title, an estimate or a project waits for a yes.
- 2026-10-19 · `apply_changes` writes nothing if any change is invalid or names something that doesn't exist, where the app's review screen drops bad lines and shows the rest. · Brief §10: "An invalid change-set writes nothing and returns the errors". A change that is already so (a step already done) is reported, not an error.
- 2026-10-19 · Titles are accepted wherever an id is expected (steps, projects, places), exact then unique prefix, ignoring case, accents and punctuation. An unknown project or place is an error, not a silent Inbox. · One call for "R: choir Monday 19:00"; an AI can't be trusted to guess ids.
- 2026-10-19 · A confirmation id lasts an hour and is re-planned when used; if the plan has changed size the person is asked again. Undo keeps, per field, the value replaced and the value written, and leaves alone fields the person changed since. Creations are undone with a tombstone. · Undo must never overwrite the person's later edit; nothing is hard-deleted.
- 2026-10-19 · Pairing: 8 characters from a 23-letter-and-digit alphabet without look-alikes (36 bits), single use, 10 minutes, stored as an HMAC under the wrap key; limits are 10 attempts a minute per IP and 60 a minute overall, and at most three live codes per space. · A code doesn't say which space it belongs to, so a wrong guess can't be counted per space; the per-space limit applies to issuing codes and completing a grant. 36 bits at 60 guesses a minute for ten minutes is about 1 in 100 million per live code.
- 2026-10-19 · The relay stores the auth secret wrapped next to the key, as the brief says, but the connector doesn't use it: it appends batches directly. · Kept only because the brief asks; dropping it is one line in `admin.ts` and leaves nothing less able.
- 2026-10-19 · The OAuth provider is built per relay address (`RELAY_URL`, a Worker variable); a grant lives while used (90 days idle); access tokens last an hour. · The protected resource needs an absolute URL; a fork sets its own.
- 2026-10-19 · The connector's node id is `connector` and its clock is kept in D1 per space, moved past every timestamp it reads (one scan of the snapshot's clocks, under 1 ms). · A phone with a fast clock must not make the connector's later writes lose.
- 2026-10-19 · `today` for the connector (`whereNow` in `src/domain/places.ts`) repeats the logic of the `where` computed in `store.ts`, which was left alone. · The store is being edited elsewhere; it should call `whereNow` when that work lands.
- 2026-10-19 · The inbox key pair is X25519, derived from the recovery key with HKDF (`inbox-x25519`) and handled with WebCrypto only (PKCS8 with a fixed prefix to import, the JWK's `x` as the public key); no `@noble/curves`. · WebCrypto can't import a raw private scalar, but PKCS8 does it with 16 fixed bytes; no new dependency to pin or audit. It needs Chrome 133, Firefox 130 or Safari 17; where X25519 is missing the drop address is hidden and the block is used.
- 2026-10-19 · A sealed box is [1][ephemeral X25519 key][IV][AES-GCM], key from HKDF over the shared secret salted with both public keys, the space id as authenticated data; the plaintext is deflate-raw JSON. · Standard ECDH-ES shape; a box can't be moved to another space, and another space's key can't open it.
- 2026-10-19 · The drop link is `/drop#<space>.<inbox key>.<z|j>.<data>`: `z` is deflate-raw base64url as the brief says, `j` is percent-encoded JSON. · **An AI can't compress or encode by hand**; only ones with a code tool can make `z`. `j` is what any AI can write (about 3× longer, so it hits the 8,000-character limit sooner). The page reads both. Product owner: this is the main risk to the one-tap link, so try it with the AI you use.
- 2026-10-19 · The drop page is a route in the SPA (`/drop`, served by the Worker's single-page-application fallback), not a second entry. · One bundle and one CSP. The relative asset paths of `base: './'` work from `/drop`, but `/drop/` (trailing slash) would break them, so the instruction says "no trailing slash". The hash router ignores the payload (it is not a view name) and the page replaces the address with the app's root once it has dealt with the link.

Open Réussite to review it." · Brief §10. A fresh Safari has no settings, so it posts to the relay address the app was built with (`VITE_RELAY_URL`); a person who typed a different relay address can't use the one-tap link from a fresh browser.

Anyone with the drop address can leave a proposal, which is only ever shown for ticking; the cap stops it filling the table.

Items that don't open with this key, or aren't a valid change-set, are deleted unseen. · Brief §10. Two devices that both fetch before either deletes can show the same drop twice; applying it on both would add the steps twice. Accepted rather than deleting on fetch, which could lose a drop if the app closes first.

Diagnostics say `state: on|off` and `last used`, never the address or a code.
- 2026-10-19 · In Sync & devices the relay's own "Connector" row shows when it was last used, not the relay's `last_seen` (which is only set when it is switched on). Turning sync off while the connector is on is allowed, with a warning that it stays on for other devices. · The row would otherwise look like a device last seen at switch-on. Switching it off needs the key, which a device that has left sync no longer holds.
- 2026-10-19 · `CHANGESET.md` gets a final section "The one-tap link"; `contractOf` cuts it from what AIs are given (the app's prompts), and `shortChangeset` already stops at the examples. · The link is for the app, and a model told about it in the command box's "JSON only" prompt could answer with a link.
- 2026-10-19 · Pasting a one-tap link into "Paste the reply" opens the review too. · A link copied on a phone is often pasted back rather than tapped.
- 2026-10-19 · The e2e relay is started with a throwaway `CONNECTOR_WRAP_KEY` and `RELAY_URL` on its command line (`playwright.config.ts`), and `relay:migrate` now targets the binding `DB`. · The connector tests need the secret; the migrate script still named the old database and stopped the e2e run.
- 2026-10-19 · There was no privacy note in About, so one was added there (relay, AI provider, loss of keys), from brief §11. · Nothing existing to extend.
- 2026-10-19 · A device snapshots after 8 batches, not 50 (`SNAPSHOT_EVERY`). · The tail is what every connector call folds (about 0.25 ms of CPU a batch): 30 batches cost 6–9 ms at today's size. For the phone it is one gzip and one upload of the list; the relay prunes the covered batches.
- 2026-10-19 · The relay's snapshot write is guarded inside its atomic batch (no stored snapshot with `upto >=` the new one; parts inserted last-first; pruning only up to a stored snapshot's `upto`). · Two writers racing past the pre-check can't leave an older snapshot over a newer one, and a batch pushed meanwhile (higher seq) is never pruned.
- 2026-10-19 · Wire generation 2 writes a document as one base clock `_h` plus `_o`, the fields whose clock differs (0 = no clock), and drops `_v` when it equals the blob's; the stored shape and `SCHEMA_VERSION` are untouched. Blobs carrying it use crypto format bytes 3 (plain) and 4 (gzip); generation 1 (1, 2) is still read. · Per-field clocks were 70% of a snapshot: the synthetic heavy list goes from 3.5 MB of JSON to 1.4 MB (474 KB to 230 KB stored), and the read from 20 ms to 9.5 ms. A new format byte, not just a field, so an app from before this change fails loudly on it (decrypt error, nothing applied) instead of skipping documents it can't read as malformed.
- 2026-10-19 · A snapshot is two blobs, hot and cold, stored as rows of kind `hot` and `cold` in `snapshots` (migration 0004 recreates the table with a `kind` column; old rows become kind `full`, one whole blob, still read by everything). Hot: open steps, steps done or (with a time) due in the last 30 days, their sessions and the last 30 days' sessions, days from 30 days ago on, every project, decision, place, stay and setting, the steps a hot day or a decision lists, and any document with a clock from the last 30 days. Cold: the rest. A document may be in both. · The connector pays CPU for what it reads, and most of a year's list is finished history. Merging is idempotent and commutative, so duplicates are harmless. The relay stays oblivious: it sees only which row is which and its `upto`.
- 2026-10-19 · Hot-only snapshots are made with the cut of the stored cold part (`{day, wall, coldUpto}`, in the encrypted hot header): hot = what is hot by that cut + everything touched since. Both parts are rewritten when no cut is known or it is a week old. The relay refuses a hot-only write (409 `bad_parts`) unless the stored cold part is at least as new as the `coldUpto` it names. · It is the one rule under which the stored cold part stays valid however long it is kept, for the app and for the connector alike, and under which a racing writer that built on an older cold part can't lose a document (checked by test). Apps need no knowledge of what is in cold.
- 2026-10-19 · The hot header carries an index of the cold ids (newline-separated per collection, about 10 bytes a finished step or session) and a digest of the cold documents: estimate points for steps done in the last 400 days and the number of steps done per project. · Without them a hot reader can't tell "new" from "in cold" for a document a batch touched, can't give the personal estimate or "N steps done" the whole list gives, and can't know a day's list points at a step it doesn't hold. The index is the one term that grows with history (a heavy year is about 40 KB, a fraction of a millisecond to read); estimate points older than 400 days weigh under 1% (half-life 60 days), so the digest is bounded.
- 2026-10-19 · The connector reads hot only for `today`, `get_day` in the hot window, `query_tasks` for open steps, `get_project`, `get_decision`, `get_task` by id and writes on open or recent things; a tool that needs cold throws `NeedCold` before writing and is run again after cold is read; titles are matched among hot steps first. A hot part that a batch since contradicts (a touched document the cold part also holds; a day or decision pointing into cold) makes the call read cold first. A test compares hot-only answers with whole-list answers with the cold rows deleted. · Correctness first: the escalation is rare and decided by data, not by guessing what a tool will touch.
- 2026-10-19 · An app that has just pushed an edit that may touch cold (an old finished step, a state change that moves a document between hot and cold, a day or decision now listing a cold step) pulls its own batch back and snapshots at once instead of after 8; the connector cuts the hot part again right after a call that had to read cold. The connector also rewrites the hot part after 10 batches on it when the hot part is under 600 documents, and otherwise leaves it to the apps. · A stub in the tail is the one thing that makes a call read cold; this keeps it to about one round trip. At the heavy size one such call costs more than the free plan's CPU, so it must not last. Rewriting the hot part (gzip) costs 17 ms at the heavy size.
- 2026-10-19 · The connector keeps the decrypted merged maps of up to 3 spaces in a module-level LRU in the isolate (24 MB by a rough count, 5 idle minutes), keyed by space and wrapped key, with the highest seq contained. A hit reads only later batches, plus a check that no newer snapshot folded batches away. Dropped when the connector is switched off or the space deleted, and when it is switched on again. Writes work on copies of the collections they change. The benchmark reports cold-isolate numbers (no cache) as the guarantee. · An earlier decision here said a cache would not help because it was as big as the snapshot and costly to write; this one lives in the isolate's memory and is never written anywhere, so a hit costs about 0.3 ms. It holds plaintext only while the connector is on, when the relay already holds the key.
- 2026-10-19 · Generation 2 blobs (format bytes 3 and 4) gzip from 1,024 bytes, not 128. · A small batch costs more CPU to gunzip than it saves: about 0.15 ms of stream set-up each, and the connector reads many.
- 2026-10-19 · The connector's `View` works out settings, the place and today once. · Profiling showed 25% of a `query_tasks` call building an Intl formatter: every printed line asked for `data`, which asked for today. The tool's own time fell from 6.8 to 2 ms (`today`) and 12.4 to 1.3 ms (`query_tasks`) at the heavy size.
- 2026-10-19 · **Result: the free plan's 10 ms is met at the owner's list size, not quite at the heavy one.** Cold isolate (no cache), median CPU, without the 1.2 ms of the MCP layer: today's list 2 to 4 ms for every call, and about 9 to 15 ms for the very first call of a fresh isolate; the heavy list 6 to 9 ms (read 6.1) and 20 to 35 ms for the first call; with a warm isolate 0.3 ms to read and 0.6 to 2.6 ms a call. What is left at the heavy size is parsing 480 KB of JSON (gunzip 1.3, parse 1.6) and rebuilding 1,100 `_hlc` objects (1.9); crypto is 0.1. Possible next steps, not taken: a column table per collection instead of repeated key names (about 40% fewer bytes), decoding `_hlc` only for documents that are merged or written, a smaller hot window. · The brief said to stop at measuring if the target could not be met at the heavy size.
