# Rule 3: Estimate every card

**The rule.** Give every card an estimate while you deal it, including the time it takes to get started.

**Grade: some support** that people misjudge how long things take, and that people with ADHD misjudge time more. **Practice only** for estimating as a way to get better: we found no test of an estimate, time and compare routine in adults with ADHD.

## What the studies found

- A review of studies on predicting how long a task will take found that underestimation was reported more often than overestimation in engineering and management studies, but not in psychology studies. It questions the often repeated finding that small tasks are overestimated and large ones underestimated, because part of it can be a statistical artefact of random error. It also lists anchoring on earlier tasks among the influences on predictions. (Halkjelsvik and Jørgensen, 2012.)
- A meta-analysis of 55 studies found that people with ADHD were worse at telling very short durations apart (under a second), more variable at several-second intervals, and less accurate at estimating and producing durations. The authors link this to an accelerated internal clock. (Marx and colleagues, 2022.)
- In three experiments, doing a short task first led people to underestimate a longer one afterwards. The authors read this as anchoring on the earlier task. Having done a task before did not necessarily make the next prediction more accurate. (Thomas, Handley and Newstead, 2007.)

## What they do not show

- The review covers mostly people without ADHD, and does not show that underestimating holds for every kind of task.
- The timing meta-analysis is about brief intervals and estimation tasks, a matter of seconds, not about estimating hours of real work. Its abstract does not separate adults from children.
- The experiments on earlier tasks are lab tasks. They do not show that estimating your own real tasks improves your later estimates.
- We found no test of estimating cards, as Réussite asks, in adults with ADHD. The search was brief.
