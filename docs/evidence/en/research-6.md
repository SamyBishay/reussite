# Rule 6: Make progress visible

**The rule.** Show progress as facts: the cards you played, a project's bar, the close of the day. No streaks, no praise, no word about what was not done. Seeing progress is the reward.

**Grade: some support** for seeing your progress, and **practice only** for the facts-only tone. Tracking progress is backed by a large meta-analysis. We found no test of the tone.

## What the studies found

- A meta-analysis of 138 randomised studies (19,951 people) found that interventions that made people check their progress more often helped them reach their goals (d = 0.40). The effect was larger when progress was written down or shared with others. (Harkin and colleagues, 2016.)
- In field experiments with a café loyalty card and a music-rating site, people sped up as they came closer to a reward. A card that started with two free stamps was finished faster than a plain one. (Kivetz, Urminsky and Zheng, 2006.)
- In an online survey of 132 adults, more ADHD symptoms went with more procrastination and a lower quality of life, and procrastination statistically linked the two. This is a correlation. (Netzer Turgeman and Pollak, 2025.)
- In a survey of 78 undergraduate men, the expected higher sensitivity to rejection was not found in the groups with ADHD. (Canu and Carlson, 2007.)

## What they do not show

- The progress meta-analysis is mostly about health and habits, not about adults with ADHD, and not about a facts-only display. The loyalty-card study is about customers and rewards, not tasks.
- We found no study we could open on streaks, on guilt or shame causing avoidance in adult ADHD, or on a display that never mentions what was not done. The no-guilt tone is a design choice, not a tested finding.
- The survey on procrastination does not mention shame. The rejection study is small and of students, and it does not support the popular claim that rejection sensitivity is part of ADHD. Figures that circulate on that claim came, in our search, only from blogs and summaries, so we did not use them.
