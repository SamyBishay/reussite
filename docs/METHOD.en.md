# The method

> **Draft for review.** Written from the rules Réussite 1 was built on. The method's author has not reviewed this text yet. Evidence IDs like `[E?]` are placeholders until `docs/evidence/` arrives (brief §15).

Réussite helps you apply a cognitive behavioural therapy (CBT) method for adults with ADHD. It doesn't replace therapy or care from a professional.

The core difficulty it addresses is **starting**. Once work has begun it usually goes fine; what fails is the jump from not doing to doing, and the jump back in after a break. Every rule below makes that jump smaller, or makes progress easier to see.

## The nine rules

1. **Write everything down.** In the app, not in your head or a chat. A to-do that lives only in memory competes with everything else for attention. `[E?]`
2. **Judge urgency and importance separately.** *Urgent* means a deadline within the urgency window (three weeks by default), or blocking something that has one. The dates handle this on their own; mark a step urgent by hand only when it blocks a deadline. A far date isn't urgent however important, and neither is anything you can't act on yet: the step is the part you control, such as asking. This gives four quadrants: **Now** (urgent and important), **Protect** (important, no clock), **Quick** (urgent, less important) and **Later** (neither). *Protect* gets a slot every day, or it never happens. `[E?]`
3. **Estimate every step while you schedule it**, including the time it takes to get started, and the planning itself. `[E?]`
4. **Time it, and write the real time down.** Comparing the estimate with the real time corrects the next estimate. Most people, and especially people with ADHD, underestimate; seeing your own ratio is how that changes. `[E?]`
5. **Break things down until the first step is simple and low-friction**, and title it with the concrete first action: the app or place, then what to do there ("Open Mail and reply to…", "Open the bank app and download…"). Watch the friction points: starting, restarting after a break, and where attention drifts. `[E?]`
6. **Make progress visible.** Played cards, progress bars, the close of the day. Seeing progress is the reward; nothing else is invented. `[E?]`
7. **Leisure, going out and seeing people are obligations**, to your mental health and to the list. Schedule them like any step, never as a prize for work. `[E?]`
8. **Decisions and problems get a method of their own**: define the problem, list every option, weigh the pros and cons of doing and of not doing each one, choose, then turn the choice into steps. See *Decisions* below. `[E?]`
9. **Goals are SMART**: specific, measurable, attainable, relevant, dated. `[E?]`

## Starters: the smallest frictionless action

Every step carries a **starter**: the single action that crosses from not doing into doing. Pick its level carefully.

- **Not below your reflexes.** Getting dressed, picking up keys, unlocking the phone: if you already do these without friction, they're not starters.
- **Not above the threshold.** Anything that needs a decision, a search, remembering something or composing text is past the entry point ("decide what to say", "find the number"). That belongs inside the step, or in a decision.
- **The right level** is the first physical or on-screen action that puts you at the task, in its place, with its material in front of you: opening the app on the right screen, sitting at the desk with the printed form and a pen, opening the front door, typing the subject line.

**The test:** could you do it in under two minutes, right now, without thinking about how? If it needs thinking, go smaller. If it's a reflex, go one step further in.

When you're stuck, the starter is the two-minute version. If the stored one didn't work, the friction is somewhere else: write a smaller starter and keep it.

## The daily loop

### Morning check-in
1. **Yesterday.** What got done, in one line. Tick the steps that were done but not marked; the rest carry over to today. A step carried three times or more is too big or hides a decision: shrink it, or turn it into a decision. No comment on what didn't happen.
2. **Today's shape.** When can you start, and what's fixed today?
3. **The plan.** Unblocked *Now* steps first, for the place you're in; then exactly one *Protect* step; then any *Quick* steps, batched. At most three work steps. One leisure or people obligation, with a time. Every item is a first step of 30 minutes or less; break bigger ones down now. Total the estimates, add the planning time, and apply your ratio.
4. **Launch points**: a start, and one or two restarts after fixed commitments. These are the moments to begin or re-enter the list, not a timetable. Steps float between them.
5. **Rest day**: the obligation only, unless something is due within two days.
6. Then start the first step.

### Insisting
Come back to the first step. Swapping it for one of the same weight is fine; skipping it isn't. If you resist, ask one question: what's in the way? A real blocker (missing information, someone else's move, a hidden decision) changes the plan: turn it into a message to send, a smaller step, or a decision. Anything else gets the starter, or a smaller one.

Common forms of avoidance, worth naming plainly when they appear: researching instead of asking the person who knows; asking for more options when there are enough; rumination; browsing or buying during a work block; tinkering with the tools instead of the list.

### Stuck in the middle of the day
Don't re-plan the day. Which step? Which friction: **can't start**, **stopped and can't get back in**, or **drifted**? Start from the resume note if there is one. Give the starter; if it already failed, write a smaller one and keep it. Restart the timer. If the same step stalls twice, it hides a decision or missing information: make that explicit, as a question to someone or a decision.

### Pausing
When you stop, leave yourself the next verb ("write the second paragraph"), so starting again takes no thought, and note why you stopped. The pattern of reasons shows where attention goes.

### Closing the day
See the cards you played and the time you logged. Choose tomorrow's first step, and optionally when to start it.

## The weekly review
Once a week, about 45 minutes, one question at a time:
1. Re-sort what changed between the quadrants.
2. Drop what no longer matters.
3. Sort the inbox into projects.
4. Make the next step of each active project a concrete first action, and give each active project a SMART goal.
5. Pick the week's *Protect* steps, and their days.
6. Name the week's obligations (people, going out, leisure), with days.
7. Queue the decisions to make, with dates.
8. Check decisions whose review date has come.

End with how the week's estimates compared with reality, and the pattern of reasons the timer stopped early.

## Decisions (balances décisionnelles)

1. **Define the problem.** What is happening; what should be happening; the gap; the constraints (money, dates, people, rules); the deadline; what is in your control. If it's really two problems, split them. Write a decision as a choice.
2. **List every option, then close the list.** Your own first. Then check for the usual missing ones: doing nothing; delaying, and until when; a smaller version; a combination; asking someone; handing it off; changing the order. Quantity first, no judging. Screen out only what is plainly impossible, and say why. The list closes after one round; reopen it only for a genuinely new fact.
3. **A decisional balance for each option**, in four columns: pros of doing it, cons of doing it, pros of not doing it, cons of not doing it. Weigh each item from 1 to 5. Count an item once when it only mirrors another. The *pros of not doing it* column is where relief and avoidance show up: that's information, not a fault.
   **Score = (pros of doing + cons of not doing) − (cons of doing + pros of not doing).**
4. **Choose, and write it down.** The highest score wins. Then test it: what must be true for this choice to be right, and what would show it's wrong, with a date to check. If the top two are within about 10%, ask one question: which would you regret less? Then decide.
5. **Turn the choice into steps**, each titled with its first action, with a starter and an estimate, and set or confirm the project's SMART goal. Put the first step on today's or tomorrow's list.
6. **Review** on the check date: did the falsifier happen? If so, start again from step 1 with the new fact.

## Tone
Facts about progress ("4 cards, 2 h 10"), never praise of character. No streaks, no guilt. One question at a time.
