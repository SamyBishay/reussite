# The matrix

The matrix sorts your open cards in four boxes, always two by two, small enough to be on a phone screen at once. Each box shows its name and how many cards it holds on one line (press and hold the name, or hover, for what it means), its first three cards as small cards (the suit and the title on up to two lines, a red dot when its date is late or near) and **View all** for the rest; every card is also in one list, folded at the bottom. Urgency and importance are judged **separately**. On the review day the **Start the review** box is at the top, above the four boxes.

On a new Réussite the matrix is not a tab at first: it arrives at twelve open cards, or after your first weekly review (see [Getting started](getting-started.md)). Until then, the deck is the fold under today's hand on Today.

## The four quadrants

- **Now** (urgent and important). A deadline within about three weeks, or something that blocks one. Today's hand starts here.
- **Protect** (important, no clock). Nothing forces these. One card gets a slot every day, or they never happen.
- **Quick** (urgent, less important). Keep them small, batch them, or hand them off.
- **Later** (neither). Park them, or drop them for good.

## Decisions

Under the four boxes (and above the folded lists) are your decisions, in compact rows grouped as **To check**, **Open** and **Decided**; the closed ones are folded under **Closed (n)**. **+ New decision** is beside the heading. See [Decisions](decisions.md).

## Urgent is a tick

*Urgent* means a deadline within about three weeks, or blocking something that has one, and **you tick it**. Réussite doesn't infer it from dates: a card due tomorrow that you haven't ticked stays in Protect or Later. Its date only orders it inside its box, nearest first.

A far date isn't urgent however important. Neither is anything you can't act on yet: the card is the part you control, such as asking.

Moving a card to another box just sets its two ticks.

**Research says:** In experiments, tasks that merely look urgent win over more important ones; the four-box method itself has not been tested. [See the evidence](research-2.md).

**Practice says:** most of what feels urgent is only loud. A real deadline is what makes it urgent.

## Moving, filtering and searching

- Every small card has a **⇄** at its end. Tap it to open the move sheet: the four boxes, then **Plan it on…** with Tomorrow and the next five days, and **Take off today** when the card is in today's hand. Moving a card is two taps, and you stay on the matrix. Tapping the card itself still opens its form. You can also drag a card to another box, or long-press it on a phone.
- **View all 5** under a box opens that quadrant's cards in a sheet, each with its buttons (the **⇄** move button, the date).
- **The search field** at the top narrows the four boxes as you type, by a card's title, short name, note, project or place. Capitals and accents do not matter ("cafe" finds "Café"), and every word you type must be there. The boxes stay; a box with no match says so. The × (or Escape) clears it.
- **All cards** at the bottom opens one list of every card, each with its quadrant named. Filter by project or suit there; the search at the top narrows this list too.
- The matrix shows the place you chose to view in the place pill (see [Places and trips](places-and-trips.md)): that place's cards and the ones marked Anywhere, or every card with **Everywhere**. (The place pill shows once you have switched on Places and trips.)
- Change a due date wherever a card appears.
- If you view one of the place's **areas**, the matrix shows the cards set to that area alone (not the place's own, not Anywhere's).
- A card that waits for another shows once, as one card: the first, with "+2 after" under its title. Tap it to unfold the rest, greyed. When the first is played, the next takes its place.

## Cards

A card's form starts with what you need most: the **title**, written as the **first action** (the app or place, then what to do there: "Open Mail and reply to…"), the **first move**, the **note**, the **due date**, the **estimate** and the **suit**. The place follows only once you have places. Urgent and important are ticks you can reach without opening More. The rest is under **More**. A card can also have a **short name** (five or six words, like "Choir · Hill House"). That is what calendars show. Leave it empty and Réussite makes one from the title.

Opening a card shows its buttons (Save, Mark played, Move to…, Break it down, Drop and the others) in a row under the title, always in reach. Under the title are **Time** and **Until**: pick a fixed time and when it ends on your phone's own clock (the length is worked out for you, "Lasts 3 h 30"; moving the start keeps it). Under **More** you will find the short name, the project, the place, the kind (a card or a decision), "Waits for", the app and link, repeats, and what a standing item needs (its weekdays and, if it ends, its last day). When something under More is set, it says so ("More · 3 set"). "Leave early" appears once there is a time.

**Research says:** Simple if-then plans help people start; no study tested a title that names the app and the action. [See the evidence](research-5.md).

**Practice says:** a title that names the app and the action is a card you can start. "Taxes" is not.

## Repeating steps and standing items

A card can repeat every day, week, month or every few days, weeks or months. A **standing item** is leisure, people or care: an obligation, not a reward. With a time and weekdays (for example, choir on Wednesdays at 19:30), it appears in your hand on those days by itself, and you're told when to leave. Make it in the morning for the evening and tonight's one is in today's hand as soon as you save it, once. To end a series, set **Until** in its More section ("until 31 December"): that is its last day, and after it nothing is placed, shown on the calendar or notified. A repeating card has the same end date in its Repeats field. Leave it empty and the series goes on until you drop it.

Standing items are not in the quadrants, because a time places them and not urgency. The matrix lists them under the grid, in a folded line called **Obligations**.
