# First-run onboarding and the AI preferences

## When it shows

`src/app/onboarding.ts`, run once in `App` after `start()` and before the first screen. It shows only when `onboardingWhy` says `new`:

1. a record of having finished: `local` key `onboarding` (`done`, `skipped` or `existing`), or `reussite.onboarded` in localStorage (a mirror, read first, so the check costs nothing);
2. a sync key on the device (`sync.key`): a device that has paired never runs it;
3. any document in any collection, `meta` (settings) included, tombstones included.

`local` is device-only: it is never synced, never in a backup. A device that passes by 2 or 3 is recorded as `existing`, so wiping its list later does not start onboarding. Playwright's `storageState` puts the localStorage mirror in every test; the onboarding tests ask for none.

## The flow

`src/flows/onboarding.tsx`, rendered by `App` in place of the tabs. No vendor is favoured anywhere in it. The first screen is the language and **three explicit choices**, each with one or two plain lines on what it gives day to day: **AI inside the app (a key)** (Plan the day and the Sunday review are a conversation inside the app; a few cents a month on the person's own key), **Your AI chat app, through the connector** (the person plans the day by talking to their AI chat app, which reads and edits the list with their yes; needs sync and an app that accepts connectors) and **No AI** (Plan the day is a few questions, one at a time; everything works without AI). Under them a link, **Any chatbot: set up by copy and paste**; then "I already use Réussite on another device" and "Skip, use defaults". There is no look screen (the default palette and the system's light or dark; the look stays in Settings → Appearance) and no cards, suits or Matrix words anywhere: the pillars are "what matters in your days". The screens of each path (`screens()`, for "Set-up · n of N", which is not shown when there is one screen):

- **No AI** (`pillars`, `places`, `done`): four plain questions, one for each pillar, each with examples and an input that holds the default pillar's name (`settings.suits`, in the current language; a renamed one's short name is `shortName` of it), then the places (at least one, the device's zone already chosen), then the end. Under a minute.
- **A key** (`key`, `chat`, `done`):
  - *The key* (`key`): the provider (OpenRouter-compatible or Anthropic, listed in that order with one neutral line: OpenRouter reaches many models with one key, Anthropic gives a key for its own models), numbered steps with the page to open, the Settings `KeyField`, "Test the connection", then **Start talking**. The provider is used at once through `draftProvider` (`src/ai/ask.ts`) and saved with the rest at the end. "I don't have a key" goes to `paste` as the any-chatbot path.
  - *The conversation* (`chat`): the first line is the app's own (`obChatFirst`), so nothing is sent until the person writes. Each turn is one `ask('setup', 'quick', …)` with `setupPrompt` (the exchange so far in order; "Show me what's sent" as ever) and the reply `{say, changes}`; `say` joins the conversation, and when `changes` holds setup changes (`readSetup`) the review opens inline. At most five exchanges, then the AI must propose. Cancel on the review goes back to the conversation, nothing written; an AI error offers "Use a chatbot instead".
- **Any chatbot** (`paste`, `done`; the link under the choices): the app builds one message, `setupPack` (`src/ai/setup.ts`: the method in short, the person's lines for an AI if any, the job, the block to end with, nothing of their list), to copy or share; the person talks in the chatbot and pastes its whole reply into the screen, read by `readReply` (the reader of Paste the reply, `src/ai/ui.tsx`) and cut back to the setup ops by `onlySetup`. Nothing is connected.
- **The connector** (`basics`, then `paste` or `pillars` and `places`, then `link`, then `done`). The connector cannot write settings (`settings.update` is the app's only, see below), so the pillars and places come first, the person's pick on `basics`: **In a chat with your AI chat app** (the same `paste` screen and `setupPack`, titled "Set up in your AI chat app", with a note that the connector cannot change settings, which is why this goes by copy and paste) or **By hand** (the by-hand questions). By hand the answers are written when the person leaves the places (`toLink`: `applyDraft`, then `applied`), not at the end: once sync is on, a restart reads the device as an existing install and the draft would be lost. Then **Connect your AI chat app** (`ConnectStep`, below). The record `finishedOnboarding(…, {connector: true})` is made for this path whatever happens, and the Next steps card shows the connector only while it is not on.
- Every screen has Back (Connect's only by hand, and only before sync is on). "Skip, use defaults" (the first screen, the basics and the by-hand ones) keeps the answers so far and goes to the end.

### Connect your AI chat app (`ConnectStep`)

Built from the Settings pieces (`Privacy`, `PairingCode` and `failure` of `src/app/ConnectorSettings.tsx`, `KeyView` of `SyncSettings.tsx`, and `setupSync`, `confirmKeySaved`, `connectorOn`, `connectorNewCode`, `refreshConnector` of `src/app/sync.ts`), in four stages:

1. **No relay** (`relayBase()` empty, a build with no `VITE_RELAY_URL` and no address typed): it says so plainly and goes on; the connector stays on the Next steps.
2. **Intro**: what the app will do, the privacy cost said first (the same two paragraphs as Settings → Connector: while it is on, whoever runs the relay can read the whole list), then **Switch on sync and the connector** (or only the connector if sync is already on) and **Not now** (to the end; the Next steps keep the line).
3. **Recovery key** (only when sync was off): `setupSync()` makes the key and starts sync; `KeyView` shows it (groups, QR, copy) and **I've saved it** (`confirmKeySaved`) or **I'll do it later** (the Next steps then list the key). Either goes on to `connectorOn()`. An error (the relay has no connector secret, a wrong key, too many tries) is said in the words of Settings and brings back the intro, whose button now only switches on the connector.
4. **Add Réussite to your app**: three steps. Copy the connector's address (shown with a copy button); add it as a custom connector in the app, with a short line for each kind of app, same weight: Claude (claude.ai or the Claude app: Settings → Connectors → Add custom connector, the steps `docs/dev/connector.md` and Settings → Connector already give) and "another app that accepts MCP connectors" (add a custom or remote connector and paste the address). No other app is given steps of its own: the repo documents none, so none are invented. Then sign in and type the pairing code (`PairingCode`: shown once, ten minutes, **New code**). A check follows: the relay's `lastUsed` (set when an app calls `/mcp`) says whether an app has come in; the screen asks every four seconds while it is open and visible, and **Check that it worked** asks at once; it says "Waiting for your app", "Your app hasn't connected yet" or "Your app is connected" with the sentence to say in a new chat ("Plan my day with Réussite"). **Continue** is always there: the connector is on whether or not an app has come in, and Settings → Connector has a new code.

`connector` (the relay's last answer) is restored at start from the cached `connector.on` (`startSync`) and refreshed in the background, so the Next steps card does not list the connector for someone who connected it, and Home's "Plan it in your AI chat" (`docs/dev/morning.md`) knows it is on.

### The setup change-set

`settings.update` (`src/ai/changeset.ts`, `docs/CHANGESET.md`): `pillars` (one to four `{name, short?}` in order, written onto the four piles in the app's language) and `preferences` (`detail`, `know`, `never`, rebuilt with `buildPreferences`). With `place.create` for the places, that is the whole set-up. `planChanges` reads the current pillars, language and preferences from `Snapshot.settings`; without it (the connector) the op comes to nothing, and the connector refuses it up front (`relay/src/connector/changes.ts`: the person's own settings are changed in the app, from a block they paste). The Writer's `settings` writes through `saveSettings`. The review shows each pillar as before → after ("Pillar 1"), and the lines for an AI. A setup reply carries no step: anything but `settings.update` and `place.create` is dropped.

### What is written, and when

By hand, answers wait in a draft and are written once, at the end, by `applyDraft`: the places (`createDoc`), then one `saveSettings` with only the keys the person touched (plus the language and, with a saved key, the AI provider). Two reasons: a device that pairs must not push defaults whose fresh clocks would win over the settings already in the space (pairing drops the draft), and a screen never touched writes nothing. What cannot wait is local anyway: the AI key (`aiKey.<provider>` in `local`), and the language, which shows at once without being saved.

With an AI, what the person ticks is applied by the review (the settings document with the pillars and lines, the places) and `settle` then writes the draft's rest at once (language, provider, `here` as the first place); the done screen only records that set-up is over. Pairing is reachable only from the first screen, so it never follows these writes.

A device that pairs ("I already use Réussite on another device", `JoinPanel`) goes straight to the end: the space has the settings and places, so nothing is asked and nothing is written. Notifications, sync and the recovery key are no longer screens; they are Home's Next steps.

### The Next steps card

`finishedOnboarding` (done, skipped or paired) records `{on: true, connector}` in `local` (`connector`: the person chose the connector path) (`connector`: the person chose the connector path) (`nextSteps`; device-only, never synced or in a backup; `loadNextSteps` reads it at start, `dismissNextSteps` clears `on`). An install from before has no record and never sees the card. `NextStepsCard` (`src/app/NextSteps.tsx`, on Home between the card and the receipt) lists `nextSteps(facts)` (`src/domain/nextSteps.ts`, pure): the recovery key to save (instead of the offer of sync), sync (when a relay is set and sync is off), reminders (when offered and not on), Addresses & people (while on), the connector (if the connector path was chosen and it is not on). Each line opens its Settings section with `openSettingsSection` (Settings, every fold around the section opened, scrolled to); a line goes when it is done and the card with it; "Hide this" removes it for good.

The pieces are the Settings ones: `KeyField` and `PreferencesEditor` (`src/ai/AiSettings.tsx`), `JoinPanel` (`SyncSettings.tsx`), and for the card `NotifySwitch` and `KeyView` stay in Settings.

## AI preferences

The setting `aiPreferences` (text, synced). Its default is the template in `docs/ai/preferences.md`, built by `src/domain/preferences.ts` (`buildPreferences`; the setup conversation fills the level of detail and two free lines through `settings.update`, and Settings → AI edits the text). Empty sends nothing. It is in the stable part of every system prompt (`systemPrompt` in `src/ai/context.ts`, after the profile and before the language line, so the bytes are the same in every feature) and in the Ask any AI and trip packs as "How I want you to work with me". "Show me what's sent" shows it as part of the system text.
