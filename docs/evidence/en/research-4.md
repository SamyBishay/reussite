# Rule 4: Time it, and compare

**The rule.** Time each card, write the real time down and compare it with the estimate. Réussite shows your own ratio of real to estimated time and uses it for the next estimates.

**Grade: practice only.** We found no test showing that timing your own tasks makes later estimates better in adults with ADHD. The one set of experiments we opened on feedback found that it did not reduce the bias.

## What the studies found

- In three experiments, people did a task and then, a week later, predicted how long a second task would take. Predictions were less biased when the second task was the same as the first. Telling people how long the first task had taken did not change the bias in any of the three experiments. (Thomas and König, 2018.)
- In another three experiments, doing a short task first made people underestimate a longer one afterwards. The authors conclude that people use earlier tasks when they predict, but that doing so does not necessarily make the prediction more accurate. (Thomas, Handley and Newstead, 2007.)

## What they do not show

- These are lab tasks, done by adults not selected for ADHD. They do not show what happens when someone times their own real cards over weeks.
- They do not show that seeing your own ratio is how estimates improve. In the one experiment that tested feedback on duration, it did not help.
- That cuts against the comparison step in that setting. We do not know whether it holds for real tasks, and we found no study of an estimate, time and compare routine in adults with ADHD.

In Réussite the comparison is a plain number you can use or ignore, with no judgement attached. That is a design choice, not a research finding.
