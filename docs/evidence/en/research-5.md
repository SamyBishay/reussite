# Rule 5: Break things down, and give every card a first move

**The rule.** Break a job down until its first card is simple, and give every card a first move: the smallest first action that puts you at it, under two minutes, with nothing to work out.

**Grade: some support.** CBT programmes that teach planning help adults with ADHD, and simple "if this, then I will" plans help people follow through. Nobody has tested the two-minute first move itself.

## What the studies found

- CBT with medication beat medication alone in a small randomised trial of 31 adults with ADHD: 56 per cent responded, against 13 per cent. (Safren and colleagues, 2005.)
- In a larger trial of 86 adults, 12 sessions of CBT gave lower symptom ratings from a blinded assessor than relaxation with educational support, after treatment and at 6 and 12 months. (Safren and colleagues, 2010.)
- A meta-analysis of 94 tests found that deciding in advance when, where and how to act, "if I meet situation Y, then I will do X", helped people reach their goals, with a medium-to-large effect (d = .65). (Gollwitzer and Sheeran, 2006.)
- A meta-analysis of 42 studies with 12,957 children found that these if-then plans helped goal achievement (g = 0.31, small to medium), and in some analyses more so for children with ADHD. (Breitwieser and Reinelt, 2026.)
- A meta-analysis of 691 correlations, in people in general, found that how unpleasant a task feels, how long the reward is delayed, low confidence and impulsiveness were among the strongest predictors of procrastination. (Steel, 2007.)

## What they do not show

- No trial we found tests a first move under two minutes, or breaking tasks down, on its own. The CBT trials test a whole programme (planning, organisation, coping with distraction), not the size of the step.
- We found no test of if-then plans in adults with ADHD. The ADHD finding is from children, and only in some analyses. The effect size from 2006 is the one that paper reports; we did not check later re-analyses of it.
- A lab study of 45 adults with ADHD against 45 other adults found that the big gap was in planning. Remembering the plan, starting it and carrying it out differed little. So it does not support the idea that starting is the main difficulty. It was a lab task, not a day's work. (Fuermaier and colleagues, 2013.)
- The procrastination review is about people in general, not ADHD.
